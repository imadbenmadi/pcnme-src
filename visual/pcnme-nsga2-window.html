NSGA-II on one PCNME window

How 40 placement plans improve over 20 generations, and how the knee plan, w and the demonstrations come out of it. Example: grid map, scenario S2, window 0, seed 1100, 294 tasks.

Key point. NSGA-II does not move plans toward the knee. It pushes all 40 plans toward the lower-left edge (the Pareto front) and spreads them along it. The knee is picked only after generation 20, from the plans on that edge.

1. One dot = one plan

A plan gives a placement to every task in the window. With 294 tasks, a plan is a list of 294 choices out of {here, Nb1, Nb2, Nb3, Nb4, cloud}:

task 1 → here  |  task 2 → Nb3  |  task 3 → cloud  |  …  |  task 294 → here

To place the dot, the simulator replays the 2 s window from the frozen state at t = 5 s with exactly these 294 placements, then computes two numbers (lower is better on both):

  • f1 = mean time cost Ctime of the 294 tasks → x-axis
  • f2 = mean energy cost Cen of the 294 tasks → y-axis

3. What happens in one generation

  1. Start of a generation: 40 plans, already scored (40 dots).
  2. Pick parents: draw two plans at random, keep the better one (lower rank; if the ranks tie, the more spread-out one). Do it again for the second parent.
  3. Crossover (probability 0.9): the child takes some of its 294 placements from parent A and the rest from parent B.
  4. Mutation (each placement with probability 1/294): about one task in the child moves to another allowed option, e.g. Nb2 → here.
  5. Repeat steps 2–4 until there are 40 children.
  6. Score the children: 40 simulations → 40 new dots.
  7. Pool: 40 old + 40 new = 80 plans.
  8. Rank: rank 1 = plans no other plan beats on both f1 and f2. Remove them; the plans now unbeaten are rank 2; and so on.
  9. Keep 40: fill by rank (rank 1 first, then rank 2, …). If a rank does not fit completely, keep its most spread-out plans (crowding distance). This is why the dots end up along the whole edge, not in one clump.

Counting: generation 1 is the starting population (40 evaluations); each of generations 2–20 adds 40 children, so 40 + 19 × 40 = 800 evaluations.

The first population starts with sensible plans: copies of the latency-greedy plan and of the energy-greedy plan with small mutations; the rest are random. Dots move down-left only because worse plans are discarded and better children replace them. No plan "walks" anywhere.

4. After generation 20

  1. Pareto front = the rank-1 plans. S2 window 0: 28 plans, 25 of them different.
  2. Knee: rescale the front's f1 and f2 to 0–1 (written f̃1, f̃2) and take the plan with the smallest f̃1 + f̃2.
  3. w of this window: from the slope of the front at the knee, the weight at which the knee is the best plan. S2 window 0: w = 0.414.
  4. w*: median of the knee weights over all windows that have a knee (27 of 36 on the grid, 22 of 36 on LuST). Grid w* = 0.414, LuST w* = 0.344.
  5. Demonstrations: the knee plan is replayed in the simulator and each of its 294 decisions is recorded as (state, action, reward, next state, Δt) for DQfD pretraining.
